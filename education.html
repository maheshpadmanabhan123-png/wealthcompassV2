The crash, replayed

Between February and March 2020, global equities fell about 34% in 33 days. A ₹10 lakh portfolio became ₹6.6 lakh on paper. Investors who did nothing were back above their starting point within five months. The ones who sold locked in the loss.

Gold, the quiet insurance

The glossary

Ask the obvious question.

There is no stupid question about your own money. Open any term below.

What is the difference between a mutual fund and an ETF?

Both pool money to buy a basket of investments. A mutual fund is bought at the end-of-day price through the fund house; an ETF trades on an exchange all day like a share. The bigger difference is cost and how you use it — not which one is "better".

What does "time in the market" actually mean?

Missing a handful of the best days can wipe out years of returns, because the best days often arrive right after the worst. Staying invested is not laziness; it is how compounding reaches you.

SIP or lump sum?

A SIP spreads entry prices over time and suits regular income. A lump sum invests everything at once and historically wins more often — if you can handle the timing risk. Most people should automate what they earn, then invest windfalls deliberately.

What is a bond, really?

A loan. You give money to a government or company; they pay interest and return the principal at maturity. Bonds stabilise a portfolio, but they are not risk-free — prices fall when interest rates rise, and issuers can default.

Why does diversification matter?

Because you cannot predict which market will lead next year. Owning assets that react differently to the same news means no single mistake or region can decide your outcome.

What is a risk profile?

A honest description of how much volatility you can accept — financially and emotionally. It is not a personality test. It is the mix you will still hold when the screen is red.

What is an expense ratio?

The annual fee a fund charges, expressed as a percentage of your money. A 1% difference sounds small until it compounds against you for twenty years.

What is a drawdown?

The fall from an investment's peak to its lowest point before recovery. A 20% drawdown needs a 25% gain just to get back to where you started — which is why avoiding the worst behaviour matters.

How does inflation affect my plan?

It quietly reduces what your money can buy. A retirement corpus that looks generous in today's rupees may feel tight in twenty years, so projections need to be inflation-adjusted, not just nominal.

What is rebalancing, in one sentence?

Bringing your portfolio back to the mix you chose by trimming what ran ahead and topping up what lagged — usually once or twice a year, and usually boring.
