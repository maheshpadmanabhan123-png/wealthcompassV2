    Growth without the currency drama

    The dirham's peg to the dollar removes one big unknown. What remains is concentration: many UAE portfolios lean heavily on property and local banks.

    • Global equity adds the diversification a local-only mix misses.
    • Gold is a store of value here, not a get-rich trade.
    • Plan end-of-service benefits as a retirement asset, not spare cash.

    Local detail: no personal income tax, but end-of-service gratuity and DIFC/ADGM savings plans need their own modelling. Compass does that.

    The world still takes its cue from here

    US markets set the tone for global risk. Right now, earnings are solid and inflation is cooling — but a handful of large companies carry a lot of the index.

    • Concentration is risk, even when it is winning.
    • 401(k), IRA and Roth accounts are part of the plan, not an afterthought.
    • Currency matters if your spending is in rupees or dirhams.

    Local detail: tax-advantaged wrappers and state taxes are modelled separately, so the plan reflects what you actually keep.

    When the dollar moves, everyone feels it

    A stronger dollar tightens conditions everywhere. For a global investor, that is both a risk and an opportunity — depending on what you own and what you spend.

    • Owning assets in several currencies is diversification, not complication.
    • Commodities and gold behave differently from shares — that is their job.
    • Know what you are hedging, and what you are simply accepting.

    Local detail: currency hedging, withholding tax and fund domicile are shown in the plan — not buried in a factsheet.
